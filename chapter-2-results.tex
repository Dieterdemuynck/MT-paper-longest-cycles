We now continue to show that there are only two candidate longest cycles, by
first showing that a longest cycle must traverse an $H_i$, and then showing
there are two types of cycles containing this $H_i$.

\paragraph{$H_i$ requirement}
If we were to remove all internal nodes and edges belonging to $H_i$, we get
two disjoint cycles. As such, the longest cycles which do not traverse any
$H_i$ are the two cycles

$$
\bigcup_{i \in \mathbb{Z}_p}D_{a,i},\;
\bigcup_{i \in \mathbb{Z}_p}D_{b,i}
$$

These cannot be the longest cycles of the graph, as one can create a longer
cycle by picking any $i$, removing the internal nodes of the path $D_{a,i}$
resp. $D_{b_i}$, and creating a new cycle through $H_i$, $D_{b,i}$ resp.
$D_{a_i}$, and $H_{i+1}$. This removes $d-2$ nodes, but introduces $2(l-2)+d$.
This cycle is larger when $d-2 < 2(\ell-2)+d$, or simply when $\ell>1$, which
follows from the condition on $\ell$ during construction. As a result, any
longest cycle must contain at least one $H_i$.

If a cycle traverses an $H_i$ path, it must enter at $a_i$ from either the
$D_{a,i}$ or $D_{a,i-1}$ paths, and leave at $b_i$ through either $D_{b,i}$ or $D_{b,i-1}$. We now show there are two types of cycles these choices create.

\paragraph{Same-side cycles}
A cycle can enter and leave an $H_i$ path on the same side, either through $D_{a,i-1}$ and $D_{b,i-1}$ or through $D_{a,i}$ and $D_{b,i}$. For simplicity, assume a cycle $C$ traverses $H_0$, $D_{a,0}$, and $D_{b,0}$. At $a_1$, this cycle now has two choices: to either traverse $H_1$, closing the cycle, or to traverse $D_{a,1}$. The latter choice leaves the cycle no other choice at $b_i$, since traversing $H_1$ would close the cycle prematurely, so $D_{b,1}$ must be traversed. We then again have the same choice at $a_2$. One can see this choice of traversing the $D$ paths as extending the closed cycle of the former choice (with length $2\ell+2d-4$) with $2d-2$ vertices. \todo{Figure}
This choice of extending repeats up until $a_{p-1}$, meaning the longest same-side cycle through $H_0$, $D_{a,0}$, and $D_{b,0}$ has a length of $2\ell-2+(p-1)(d-1)$, and traverses all vertices

$$
\bigcup_{i \in \mathbb{Z}_p \setminus \{p-1\}}V(D_{a,i})
\;\cup\;
\bigcup_{i \in \mathbb{Z}_p \setminus \{p-1\}}V(D_{b,i})
\;\cup\; 
V(H_0)
\;\cup\; 
V(H_{p-1})
$$

The reasoning for $D_{a,-1}$ and $D_{b,-1}$, and for other $H_i$ paths, is analogous. Note that if this type of cycle is the longest cycle of the graph, all vertices $a_i$ and $b_i$ meet all longest cycles.

\paragraph{Opposite-side cycles}
A cycle can also enter and leave an $H_i$ path on opposite sides, either through $D_{a,i-1}$ and $D_{b,i}$ or through $D_{a,i}$ and $D_{b,i-1}$.
